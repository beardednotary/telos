# Sector header illustrations

Five landscape headers, one per sector, to sit above the sector's panel the
way `camp.webp` sits on the outpost screen.

These exist to solve a writing problem, not a decoration one. Mosswood and
Blackstone are described functionally - "Overgrown perimeter. Low risk,
quick returns." - while the voice only switches on at Sector 03. A header
each gives the early game character on day one, which is exactly where the
first round of player feedback said it was missing.

## Rules for all five

Derived from `../camp-study/art-prompt.md`, which is the style lock. Keep
the technique, palette and negative list identical across every sector or
they stop reading as one world.

- **Aspect:** 16:9 landscape. Drawn at full screen width and cropped to
  roughly 2.3:1, so keep the subject central and leave headroom.
- **No figures**, or one small distant figure for scale only. These are
  places. The people live in the camp illustration.
- **Deliver at 1290px wide, WebP q86.** That is full width on the largest
  phone at 3x. Budget about 230 KB each.
- **The Spire is a spoiler.** It is the one image the whole game points at.
  Hold it back from any store listing or sector list thumbnail.

## Shared prompt skeleton

> Use case: stylized-concept. Asset type: a single still illustration for a
> mobile incremental expedition RPG's sector header, wide 16:9 landscape
> composition. **SCENE GOES HERE.** Art style: confident hand-inked shapes,
> sparse woodcut hatching, dark graphic silhouette masses against muted bone
> paper, like a considered pen-and-ink indie RPG rulebook illustration.
> Restrained palette charcoal #20251F, moss-grey, parchment #E8E1CE, tiny
> muted ochre accent. Strong, readable shapes at phone size. Crisp shapes,
> generous negative space, subject occupying the central band. Background
> sparse, no busy environment. No text, typography, UI, panel borders,
> numbers, watermarks, glowing magic, photorealism, glossy 3D, anime faces,
> or decorative fantasy ornament. No people. This is art direction for a
> place, not a character study; no animation.

## The five scenes

Every detail below is taken from the sector's own blurb, journal lines and
prior-survey records. Nothing here invents world content.

### 01 Mosswood Verge
> An overgrown forest perimeter at the edge of cleared ground. A disused
> service road graded flat runs through it. Squat timber survey posts stand
> along the road with marks cut into their faces. A collapsed lattice relay
> mast lies half-swallowed by growth. Low mist, wet ground, quiet.

### 02 Blackstone Hollow
> A collapsed mine's surface works. A leaning timber headframe over a shaft
> mouth, heavy re-cut shoring, a low winch house with a shuttered window,
> and equipment stacked and sheeted under canvas. Spoil heaps and old rail.

### 03 The Cinder Archive
> The interior of a burned library that never finished burning. Tall stone
> bookcases still standing in rows, charred but upright, their shelves dense
> with ledgers. Thin smoke rising through gaps in the floor from somewhere
> below. One heavy sealed reading-room door, still shut.

### 04 Riftline Descent
> A narrow vertical split in bare rock dropping out of sight. Rope anchors
> and rigging set down the whole visible depth, stepped ledges to one side,
> depth marks cut into the wall at intervals. No vegetation, no sky.

### 05 Spire of Telos
> A tall narrow unfinished tower on a rise, seen from below. Each stage of
> its height is visibly different masonry, as though built by different
> hands centuries apart. The top course is unfinished: cut stone stacked
> ready beside it, scaffolding still standing, nothing above.

Note: the camp illustration already carries "a distant narrow unfinished
tower" on its horizon, so the Spire is established before it is reached.
Keep the two consistent.
