TELOS / TYPE & COLOR / STUDY 04

The same game. Three different voices.

The flow is settled. These change the lettering, palette and small surface details.
The illustration, information and decisions stay the same.

01 / COOL & PRACTICAL

Survey Office

Fog gray, slate ink, a rust-colored marker.

Barlow Semi Condensed + Barlow. Clear, compact titles feel like signs on field equipment. Cool surfaces let the camp illustration supply the warmth.

The most practical option. Its game identity relies on the artwork, equipment and world-specific language.

02 / DARK & MEASURED

Night Watch

Charcoal, pale lettering, restrained brass.

IBM Plex Sans Condensed + IBM Plex Sans. Precise lettering and dark framed surfaces give the numbers a tactical setting. Brass is reserved for the action, progress and finds.

The strongest game-menu direction. It feels more serious; dark surfaces need careful contrast and clear boundaries.

Try the full loop →

03 / PRINTED & STRANGE

Woodcut

Stone white, black ink, oxblood.

Grenze + Source Sans 3. Sharp, compact serif titles borrow the character of printed fantasy books. Controls stay plain; the expressive lettering belongs to people, places and objects.

The clearest tabletop-RPG flavor. More overtly fantasy, with a title face that benefits from restraint.

Try the full loop →

The recommendation: start with Night Watch if the missing quality is “this feels like a game.” Choose Survey Office for a lighter field-instrument feel, or Woodcut for the strongest fantasy voice.

All three preserve JetBrains Mono for quantities. No palette can certify who made an interface; the useful test is whether the choices feel specific to Telos.
