# Salvage illustrations

All nineteen gear items. The seven epics were drawn first, because rarity
should feel different and those are the ones people remember finding; the
remaining twelve follow below.

They exist for one moment: the salvage beat in the debrief, where "and
something else came back with them" currently pays off with a code-drawn
engraving. That is the beat the whole return screen builds to.

## Where they are used, and where they are not

**The reveal, yes. The lists, no.** `GearMark` stays everywhere equipment
is being compared or scanned, because the test from the design direction
still applies: *each illustration should answer a question.* A drawn rig
at the reveal answers "what did we find." The same drawing in a comparison
row answers nothing the numbers were not already saying, and makes a list
slower to read.

## Rules for all seven

Same style lock as `../camp-study/art-prompt.md` and the sector headers.
Diverge on any of it and the salvage stops belonging to the world the
sectors are drawn in.

- **Square, 1:1.** These sit in a small frame, not a banner.
- **One object, centred, on bone paper.** No scene, no hands, no figures,
  no background environment. A plate from a catalogue, not a still life.
- **Worn, used, repaired.** Everything in this world has been carried.
- **Deliver at 640px, WebP q86.** About 40 KB each.
- **No glow.** The lock bans glowing magic; the only warm note permitted
  is the same muted ochre as the camp lantern, and only where the object
  genuinely holds a flame or a light.

**Three of these are spoilers.** `spire_sigil`, `telos_lens` and
`last_charter` only exist at the end of the game. Keep them out of store
listings, galleries and any preview that is not the player's own debrief.

## Shared prompt skeleton

> Use case: stylized-concept. Asset type: a single still object study for a
> mobile expedition RPG's salvage reveal, square 1:1 composition, the object
> centred with generous margin. **OBJECT GOES HERE.** Art style: confident
> hand-inked shapes, sparse woodcut hatching, dark graphic silhouette masses
> against muted bone paper, like a plate from a considered pen-and-ink indie
> RPG rulebook. Restrained palette charcoal #20251F, moss-grey, parchment
> #E8E1CE, tiny muted ochre accent. Worn, used, field-repaired. Strong
> readable silhouette at small size. No scene, no background environment, no
> hands, no people. No text, typography, UI, panel borders, numbers,
> watermarks, glowing magic, photorealism, glossy 3D, or decorative fantasy
> ornament. This is a catalogue plate of one object; no animation.

## The seven

### first_light — FIRST LIGHT
*"It was down there before there was a down there."* — Riftline Descent
> A squat stone lamp of a shape nobody makes any more, cut from pale rock

### spire_sigil — SPIRE SIGIL *(spoiler)*

### telos_lens — TELOS LENS *(spoiler)*

# The remaining twelve

Commons and uncommons are the harder half, and worth knowing that before
starting. A brass sensing rig has mechanism to draw; a frayed cord does
not. **Resist the urge to make a common item more interesting than it is.**
A piece of worn rope rendered as an intricate artefact breaks the economy
of the whole set - rarity has to be visible at a glance, and the cheapest
way to signal it is restraint.

Rough guide for how much is on the plate:

| Rarity | What the plate carries |
|---|---|
| Common | One object. Plain, worn, no mechanism. Lots of paper around it |
| Uncommon | One object with a single working part - a lens, a hinge, a strap |
| Rare | One object with real mechanism, or something that is visibly wrong |
| Epic | Already drawn. Mechanism, a stand, or an obvious impossibility |

Same skeleton, same lock, same 640px square WebP as the seven above.

# The caravan's six

Caravan salvage (`kCaravanGear` in `lib/data/content.dart`) is found in no
sector. It comes from a supply line that has run the old service road for
longer than the outpost has existed. These pieces should look like freight
gear: teamsters' kit, made to be loaded, lashed down and checked at gates.
Compared with sector salvage, they are **plainer and more standardised,
like things issued and stamped rather than found**. That difference is how
someone recognises a caravan find before reading its name.

Same skeleton, same style lock, same rarity guide and same 640px WebP as
above. Use `camp-concept.png` and `ore_sense.webp` as references, exactly
as the twelve did.

Two lessons from the first nineteen that apply here:

- **Brass drifts gold.** Deep Lamp needed a revision because its metal came
  out warm. Put the colour-discipline line in the first prompt; don't rely
  on the revision.
- **Paper and dials invite text.** Three of these have a stamp, a dial or a
  label. Every mark must stay a mark: no letters, no numerals, nothing
  readable.

None of the six are spoilers.

### waybill: TEAMSTER'S WAYBILL · uncommon
*"Stamped for a place that was not here yet."*
> A single folded freight waybill of heavy creased paper, tied through a
> brass-ringed eyelet to a short loop of string, the way a tag is tied to
> cargo. One round inked stamp impression on its face, partly worn away.

Constraints: the eyelet and string are the one working feature. The stamp
is an abstract round mark of rings and a simple cross-hatch, with no
letters or numbers. Ruled lines appear only as faint marks. Paper parchment,
stamp charcoal. No warm accent.

### road_lantern: ROAD LANTERN · uncommon
*"Lit at the gate. Never put out on the road."*
> A plain tin hurricane lantern of the cheap, common kind: a squat fuel
> font, two thin wire guards around a short glass chimney, and a top cap
> with a wire carrying bail. Dented, its paint mostly worn off. Lit, with a
> very small low flame.

Constraints: deliberately simpler and cheaper than the Deep Lamp. Use wire
guards instead of mesh, with no brass cap or base. The flame is the only
warm note: a tiny flat ochre shape with no glow, halo or light cast on the
glass or paper. All metal stays neutral grey. It must read as a different
object from the Deep Lamp at 88px: squat and wide where the Deep Lamp is
tall and narrow.

### axle_pin: AXLE PIN · rare
*"Turned by hand. It fits every wagon on the line."*
> A heavy hand-forged iron linchpin, long and slightly tapered, with a flat
> forged head. A split cotter pin runs through a hole near its tip, with
> one leg bent back to lock it. Worn smooth and bright in a band where a
> wheel hub has turned against it for years.

Constraints: the cotter through the pin is the mechanism. Show the pin
lying at a slight diagonal so its length and the cotter both read. The
worn band is parchment-pale against charcoal iron, not shiny. Add nothing
else: no wheel, no hub, no chain. No warm accent.

### drover_coat: DROVER'S COAT · rare
*"Proofed against weather with something that still smells of smoke."*
> A heavy oilskin drover's coat, folded square the way it would be packed
> on a wagon seat, with a short shoulder cape lying over the fold. One horn
> button is visible, and beside it a mismatched replacement. A long
> darkened, smoke-stained patch runs along one edge of the hem.

Constraints: a folded garment, with no figure, mannequin or hanger. The
oddity is the smoke staining: a dark tonal patch, with no burns, holes or
flames. Use broad dark silhouette masses for the oilskin and selective
hatching on the fold lines. No warm accent.

### slow_watch: THE SLOW WATCH · epic
*"Eleven years behind, and never once late."*
> A heavy old pocket watch on a short length of chain, its back case
> hinged open to show the movement: a few large gears and a balance wheel.
> The front dial carries tick marks only, and its two hands sit at an
> ordinary-looking time. The case is worn smooth at the edges from years
> in a pocket.

Constraints: **no numerals on the dial**. Use tick marks only, with a
heavier mark at each quarter. The open back is the epic's mechanism, so
keep it legible at 88px by using a few big gears rather than a dense
movement. The metal is tarnished neutral grey, never gold. This is the
lead driver's watch from the manifest. It echoes the eleven-year-old tins
in Mosswood, so don't make it look precious or magical. It should look
like a working tool that has never been corrected. No warm accent.

### gate_seal: GATE SEAL · epic
*"The caravan stops where this is pressed. Nowhere else."*
> A freight seal: a heavy disc of soft grey lead crimped shut around the
> two ends of a twisted wire loop, the way cargo and gates are sealed. Its
> face is pressed with a deep, crisp, simple mark. Beside it lies the
> pliers-like iron sealing press that made the impression, its jaws still
> open.

Constraints: two objects is the epic allowance here. The press is the
mechanism, and it shows how the seal is made. Keep the lead seal clearly
the subject: larger, central, with the press set behind and to one side.
The pressed mark is a simple geometric sign, a ring around a short
vertical bar like a gate post. It is not a letter or a heraldic crest, and
it must not resemble the Spire Sigil's tower or the Quiet Seal's wax. Lead
is dull grey, never silver or shiny. No warm accent.

## A caravan scene (optional)

The caravan itself has no illustration yet. The camp card and the
debrief's "With the caravan" beat are text only. A scene would make it a
place in the world rather than a mechanic. It would need a small code
change to show it, in the same way `SectorHeader` shows sector art.

Use the sector-header skeleton and rules: 16:9, delivered at 1290px wide,
subject in the central band because it is cropped to about 2.3:1. Use the
same palette and negative list.

> A short line of three covered freight wagons halted at a plain timber
> outpost gate in the grey first light of morning. Canvas tilts lashed down
> over cargo, a hurricane lantern hung from the lead wagon's seat, mules or
> heavy horses standing in harness with heads down. The old graded service
> road runs on past the gate into low mist and a treeline. Squat survey
> posts with marks cut into their faces stand at the roadside. One small
> distant figure at the gate, for scale only.

Constraints: the lantern flame is the only warm note, tiny and flat, with
no glow. Keep the gate, wagons and road continuous with Mosswood's header
(the same service road and survey posts) and with the camp illustration.
No text on the wagon canvas, the gate or the posts.
