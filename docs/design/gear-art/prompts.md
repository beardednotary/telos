# Salvage illustrations

All nineteen gear items. The seven epics were drawn first, because rarity
should feel different and those are the ones people remember finding; the
remaining twelve follow below.

They exist for one moment: the salvage beat in the debrief, where "and
something else came back with them" currently pays off with a code-drawn
engraving. That is the beat the whole return screen builds to.

## Where they are used, and where they are not

**The reveal, yes. The lists, no.** `GearMark` stays everywhere equipment
is being compared or scanned, because the test from the design direction
still applies: *each illustration should answer a question.* A drawn rig
at the reveal answers "what did we find." The same drawing in a comparison
row answers nothing the numbers were not already saying, and makes a list
slower to read.

## Rules for all seven

Same style lock as `../camp-study/art-prompt.md` and the sector headers.
Diverge on any of it and the salvage stops belonging to the world the
sectors are drawn in.

- **Square, 1:1.** These sit in a small frame, not a banner.
- **One object, centred, on bone paper.** No scene, no hands, no figures,
  no background environment. A plate from a catalogue, not a still life.
- **Worn, used, repaired.** Everything in this world has been carried.
- **Deliver at 640px, WebP q86.** About 40 KB each.
- **No glow.** The lock bans glowing magic; the only warm note permitted
  is the same muted ochre as the camp lantern, and only where the object
  genuinely holds a flame or a light.

**Three of these are spoilers.** `spire_sigil`, `telos_lens` and
`last_charter` only exist at the end of the game. Keep them out of store
listings, galleries and any preview that is not the player's own debrief.

## Shared prompt skeleton

> Use case: stylized-concept. Asset type: a single still object study for a
> mobile expedition RPG's salvage reveal, square 1:1 composition, the object
> centred with generous margin. **OBJECT GOES HERE.** Art style: confident
> hand-inked shapes, sparse woodcut hatching, dark graphic silhouette masses
> against muted bone paper, like a plate from a considered pen-and-ink indie
> RPG rulebook. Restrained palette charcoal #20251F, moss-grey, parchment
> #E8E1CE, tiny muted ochre accent. Worn, used, field-repaired. Strong
> readable silhouette at small size. No scene, no background environment, no
> hands, no people. No text, typography, UI, panel borders, numbers,
> watermarks, glowing magic, photorealism, glossy 3D, or decorative fantasy
> ornament. This is a catalogue plate of one object; no animation.

## The seven

### ore_sense — ORE-SENSE RIG
*"It hums before you see the seam."* — Blackstone Hollow
> A miner's sensing harness: a shoulder yoke of worn leather straps carrying
> a brass housing with a single small dial, coils of copper wire bound along
> one strap, a short probe on a cord. Built by hand, repaired more than once.

### quiet_seal — QUIET SEAL
*"Pressed into wax that never set."* — The Cinder Archive
> An archivist's seal matrix: a short turned handle above a heavy engraved
> brass die, resting beside a pressed disc of dark wax that is still soft and
> slumping at its edge, the impression in it not quite holding.

### null_core — NULL CORE
*"Instruments read zero. All of them."* — Riftline Descent
> A dense dark sphere the size of a fist, utterly matte and featureless,
> held in a cage of thin brass meridians on a small stand. Two tiny gauges
> fixed to the cage, their needles resting hard at the bottom of their scales.

### first_light — FIRST LIGHT
*"It was down there before there was a down there."* — Riftline Descent
> A squat stone lamp of a shape nobody makes any more, cut from pale rock
> worn smooth by handling, a shallow bowl at its top holding a small steady
> flame. The only warmth in the image, muted ochre, no halo or radiance.

### spire_sigil — SPIRE SIGIL *(spoiler)*
*"Proof of arrival."* — Spire of Telos
> A cast token the size of a palm, edges softened by carrying, bearing a
> simple incised mark of a narrow tower in low relief. A punched hole at one
> edge with a short length of cord through it.

### telos_lens — TELOS LENS *(spoiler)*
*"Shows the end of the thing you are looking at."* — Spire of Telos
> A single thick lens in a tarnished brass mount with a folding handle. The
> glass is ground in several concentric rings rather than one smooth curve,
> as though corrected repeatedly by different hands.

### last_charter — THE LAST CHARTER *(spoiler)*
*"The guild, in its own hand."* — Spire of Telos
> A single folded sheet of heavy paper, creased and weathered, weighted flat
> by a rough cut stone laid across one corner. Ruled lines and a blank
> signature line visible as marks only, never as readable writing.

# The remaining twelve

Commons and uncommons are the harder half, and worth knowing that before
starting. A brass sensing rig has mechanism to draw; a frayed cord does
not. **Resist the urge to make a common item more interesting than it is.**
A piece of worn rope rendered as an intricate artefact breaks the economy
of the whole set - rarity has to be visible at a glance, and the cheapest
way to signal it is restraint.

Rough guide for how much is on the plate:

| Rarity | What the plate carries |
|---|---|
| Common | One object. Plain, worn, no mechanism. Lots of paper around it |
| Uncommon | One object with a single working part - a lens, a hinge, a strap |
| Rare | One object with real mechanism, or something that is visibly wrong |
| Epic | Already drawn. Mechanism, a stand, or an obvious impossibility |

Same skeleton, same lock, same 640px square WebP as the seven above.

## Mosswood Verge

### scav_cord — SCAVENGER CORD · common
*"Frayed, but it holds."*
> A coil of waxed rope, laid flat in a loose spiral. One end whipped with
> thread, the other frayed open and splaying. Nothing else.

### mossglass — MOSSGLASS SHARD · common
*"Green where it should be clear."*
> A single flat shard of thick broken glass, edges chipped, lying on its
> side. Clouded through its body rather than on its surface.

### field_optic — FIELD OPTIC · uncommon
*"One cracked lens, still true."*
> A short brass monocular with a knurled focus ring, scuffed from carrying.
> A single clean crack runs across the front lens, corner to corner.

### verge_charm — VERGE CHARM · rare
*"Nobody remembers who carved it."*
> A small flat token of pale wood on a worn thong, carved with a mark nobody
> uses any more. Handled until the carving is nearly smooth.

## Blackstone Hollow

### hollow_pick — HOLLOW PICK · common
*"Worn to the shape of a hand."*
> A short miner's hand pick, head dulled and chipped, the wooden haft worn
> into a shallow grip where it has been held for years.

### slag_brace — SLAG BRACE · uncommon
*"Heavy. Worth it."*
> A thick forearm brace of blackened iron plate over leather, buckled with
> two heavy straps. Dented across one face.

### deep_lamp — DEEP LAMP · rare
*"Burns on something that is not oil."*
> A miner's safety lamp: a brass base and cap with a tall mesh-guarded glass
> between them, a wire bail over the top. The flame inside is small, steady
> and the wrong colour - pale, almost white. Muted ochre only, no halo.

## The Cinder Archive

### ash_ledger — ASH LEDGER · common
*"Half the pages are still readable."*
> A bound ledger lying closed, its cover scorched and its page block burned
> away unevenly along one edge. Ruled lines visible as marks only, never as
> readable writing.

### archivist_optic — ARCHIVIST OPTIC · uncommon
*"Ground for reading, not for seeing."*
> A reading glass with an unusually shallow, wide lens in a thin brass rim,
> on a short flat handle made to rest on a page rather than be held up.

### cinder_key — CINDER KEY · rare
*"Warm to the touch, always."*
> A long iron key with a complicated ward, its bit blackened as though it has
> been in a fire. The metal is faintly discoloured along its length, the way
> iron is after heat.

## Riftline Descent

### rift_anchor — RIFT ANCHOR · uncommon
*"Holds to nothing and does not move."*
> A forged steel anchor bolt with a wide eye, set into nothing - no rock, no
> plate, no fixing. It sits flat on the paper as though the surface it was
> driven into has been removed.

### descent_rig — DESCENT RIG · rare
*"Rated for depths nobody has measured."*
> A rope descender: a figure-of-eight steel plate strung on a worn line,
> with a locking carabiner through the upper eye. Scored bright along the
> bends where the rope has run through it.
